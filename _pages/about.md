I am an Associate Professor (Docent) in Meteorology at the Department of Earth Sciences, Uppsala University. My research focuses on **air–sea interaction**, **ocean surface waves**, **marginal ice zone physics**, and **polar meteorology**, with the overarching goal of improving the representation of these coupled processes in Earth System Models.

My work spans from field observations and remote sensing to high-resolution numerical modelling. A central theme is understanding how ocean surface waves — particularly swell waves — modify the exchange of momentum, energy, and greenhouse gases across the sea surface, and how these processes feed back into the atmospheric boundary layer, ocean circulation, and Arctic sea ice dynamics. A major current focus is the **marginal ice zone**: how waves interact with and attenuate through sea ice, how turbulent fluxes at the air–sea–ice interface drive polar weather systems such as polar lows, and how to represent these physical processes in coupled Earth System Models.

I completed my PhD at Uppsala University in 2017 under the supervision of Prof. Anna Rutgersson and Dr. Erik Sahlée, with a thesis titled _Introducing Surface Gravity Waves into Earth System Models_. I have held visiting positions at the University of Miami, Johns Hopkins University, the Norwegian Meteorological Institute, the Swedish Meteorological and Hydrological Institute (SMHI), and the University of Reading.

My group includes PhD students and postdocs working on wave–ice interactions, polar lows, atmospheric rivers, and air–sea turbulent fluxes. I am PI on grants from the Swedish Research Council (VR), Formas, Carl Tryggers Stiftelse, the Crafoord Foundation, and Uppsala University, and a co-applicant on the Horizon Europe [GreenFeedBack](https://greenfeedback.eu/) project.
