Polar lows are small, intense maritime cyclones that form over open water at high latitudes, often near the sea-ice edge. They bring hurricane-force winds and heavy snowfall, and they are difficult to forecast because they depend on strong, small-scale exchanges of heat and moisture with the underlying ocean.

We use coupled atmosphere–wave–ocean–ice models to understand how sea surface temperature anomalies, sea spray and the ocean's thermal structure influence the development of polar lows, how polar lows in turn cool and mix the ocean beneath them, and how their frequency and intensity may change in a warmer climate. Related work looks at atmospheric rivers and explosive cyclones in the North Atlantic, and at how ocean waves affect storm tracks and development at mid-latitudes.
