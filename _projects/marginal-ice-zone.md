The marginal ice zone is the band between open ocean and consolidated pack ice, where waves, broken floes, open water and the atmosphere all interact. It is expanding as Arctic sea ice retreats, and it is one of the least well represented regions in Earth System Models.

We study how ocean waves propagate into and decay within sea ice, how the presence of waves and ice changes the momentum and turbulent fluxes between the atmosphere, the ice and the ocean, and how these fluxes should be conserved when the components of an Earth System Model are coupled. The work draws on multi-platform field campaigns, wave buoys deployed in the ice, satellite remote sensing and coupled atmosphere–wave–ocean–ice modelling.

Current projects:

- _Momentum flux conservation across the air–sea–ice interface within the marginal ice zone in Earth System Models_ — Formas Career Grant, 2025–2028 (PI).
- _Turbulence fluxes across the air–sea–ice interface in the marginal ice zone and their influence on polar cyclones_ — Swedish Research Council, 2025–2028 (PI).
- _Wave–ice interactions in the marginal ice zone_ — Carl Tryggers Stiftelse, 2025–2027 (PI).
